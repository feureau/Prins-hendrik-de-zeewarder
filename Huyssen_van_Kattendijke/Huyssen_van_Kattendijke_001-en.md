## Stay in Makassar

March 7, 1837

It was in Fort Rotterdam that Prince Hendrik met Diponegoro, about whom he wrote candidly in his 'diary'[^90], as well as in a letter to his father:

> 'Having seen this building (the church), we proceeded to the residence of Diepo Negoro, our former enemy in Java and now our prisoner. It is perhaps dangerous to lift the veil that lies over this history. But I dare it. Everyone knows that Diepo Negoro was in rebellion against us. But his capture will always, in my view of the matter, be a stain on the old Dutch honor. It is true he was a mutineer, but he came to bring an end to a war that had cost so many people, both to us and to him, and what is more, he came to negotiate trusting in Dutch honor. Then he was seized on the orders of General de Kock. I believe that this matter, which served us very well indeed (regarding our possession of the whole of Java), has done us the greatest harm in morale, for if we should unfortunately have war in Java again, one of the two will perish—either we or the Javanese—for no leader will ever want to have anything to do with us again. This will happen not only in Java but everywhere. I am convinced that the reason for the non-surrender of the campong Bonjol on Sumatra is nothing other than that the leader of the Bonjolese says to himself: if I negotiate with the Dutch, I am sure to be treated like Diepo Negoro'.

During the meeting, Diponegoro took the prince with him to show him 'his miserable residence', which consisted of two small rooms perhaps eight feet square. Diponegoro spent most of his time copying the 'Koran'. 'His pride is much diminished; whereas previously he spoke nothing but Javanese, later, when he transitioned to speaking Malay, he always said *loe* (in Malay even more common than the Dutch *jouw*), something which is only used toward native servants and which Europeans consider an insult'.

In the letter to his father, Prince Hendrik added: 'Il a une figure très animée et on voit qu'il est encore rempli de feu' [He has a very animated face and one sees that he is still full of fire] and further expressed his indignation about the system of forced deliveries: *A Java Mr van den Bosch a réintroduit l'ancien système c'est à dire si par exemple on veut avoir 100 Livres de caffé on le dit aux Chefs et alors ces chefs, oui il est vrai donnent ce qu'on leur a demandé, mais on est sur que le Javanais lui a livré 200 Livres ce qui les irrite et les vexe d'une manière horrible*.[^91]
