March 10, 1837

Dear and Good Papa!

It is from Macassar that I address these few lines to you to give you our news. I shall therefore begin by sending you my congratulations on the occasion of Grand Papa's birthday, adding my best wishes for his happiness and prosperity. Dear and Good Papa, please have the kindness to give my compliments and affection to my brothers, my sister, and my cousins if you are in Russia.

Before I forget, Dear Papa, Mr. Avrein has asked me to tell you that he will probably take the return route he indicated regarding the Moluccas, because all the precursors of a change in the monsoon are appearing, which forces us to make as much progress toward the East as possible.

From the first day, while going to see the Fort here, I saw our unfortunate prisoner Djeppo Negoro, who fell into our hands purely through betrayal. He came to me immediately, extending his hand, and, having led me to his room on the first floor, he told the Governor here, Monsieur Bousquet, that it gave him great pleasure that someone came to see his miserable dwelling. Otherwise, he was quite cheerful; he laughed, but it is said that this cheerfulness was feigned, or that it was not natural. Despite that, he is very proud; even before, he refused to speak in Malay. He has a very shrewd face and one can see that he is still full of fire.

Among the things I have seen here were the prisons, but the heat in there is horrible; I suppose this is because each cell is lined with wood on the inside.

I have heard it said that one cannot trust the natives of the western coasts of Celebes, but one cannot be surprised by this when one knows how much they have been mistreated in the past. In Java, Mr. van den Bosch has reintroduced the old system; that is to say, if, for example, one wants 10 pounds of coffee, it is told to the Chiefs and then these chiefs—yes, it is true—give what was requested, but it is certain that the Javanese delivered 200 pounds to them, which irritates and vexes them in a horrible manner.

Farewell Dear Papa, we leave tomorrow. I remain forever your most respectful and tenderly attached son,

Hari

Macassar, this 10th of March 1837.
