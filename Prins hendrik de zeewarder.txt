**Prince Henry of the Netherlands** (*Willem Frederik Hendrik*, 1820–1879), nicknamed *"de Zeevaarder"* (the Navigator), played a distinct role in 19th-century Dutch maritime, political, and colonial history.

---

### Key Historical & Personal Profile

* **Identity:** Son of King William II of the Netherlands and Grand Duchess Anna Pavlovna of Russia; younger brother to King William III.
* **Naval Career:** Entered the Royal Netherlands Navy at an early age. His long maritime service and advocacy for Dutch shipping earned him the epithet *"de Zeevaarder"* (the Navigator).
* **Governance (1850–1879):** Served for nearly 30 years as the Governor / Lieutenant-Representative (*Stadhouder*) of the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg on behalf of his brother, King William III.
* **Philanthropy & Legacy:** Founder and patron of maritime charities, most notably the *Prins Hendrik Stichting* in Egmond aan Zee—a dedicated retirement home and sanctuary for merchant navy seafarers.

### The March 7, 1837 Meeting with Prince Diponegoro

On March 7, 1837, while serving as a 16-year-old naval midshipman aboard the frigate *Bellona* in the Dutch East Indies, Prince Henry visited the exiled Javanese leader **Prince Diponegoro** at **Fort Rotterdam** in Makassar.

* **The Encounter:** Prince Henry spent approximately an hour visiting Diponegoro in his quarters inside the fort.
* **The Prince's Reflections:** In his personal travel log and a March 10 confidential letter to his father, Prince Henry expressed deep sympathy for Diponegoro. He explicitly observed that the Javanese prince had been captured dishonorably through Dutch treachery under a false flag of truce at Magelang (1830).
* **Scholarly Access:** While the original handwritten logbooks reside in the private Royal House Archives (*Koninklijk Huisarchief*) in The Hague, the complete transcripts and translations of these diary entries are published and freely available online in **Peter Carey's 2022 Open-Access study, *"Outsider Insiders: Four Officers' Exile Conversations with Diponegoro, 1830–1837"***.
